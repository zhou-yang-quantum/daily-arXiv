## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 1

### Time-dependent integrable quantum field theories with dynamical boundaries
**Pradip Kattel — arXiv:2610.03870**

Kattel constructs driven chiral quantum field theories on a finite interval from the scattering and reflection matrices of a time-independent integrable model. Requiring collisions at the same time to have position-independent scattering forces the spectral coordinates to be affine, with a common slope for left- and right-moving particles. Consequently, the bulk interactions and boundary couplings must follow coordinated time dependence.

The construction combines local consistency of collisions and reflections with consistency around complete boundary round trips. The latter produces boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations. Matching one- and two-particle dynamics reconstructs a local Hamiltonian; solving the boundary equations then supplies the driven many-body wavefunction. Examples include rational SU(N) models and an O(3)-invariant model with dynamical boundary impurities and driven Kondo couplings. The method permits a constrained family of drives, rather than arbitrary modulation.

**Background.** Factorized scattering reduces many-particle collisions to compatible two-particle processes. The Yang–Baxter equation enforces collision consistency, while reflection equations handle boundaries. Boundary qKZ equations organize the additional consistency of repeated reflections.

**Why it matters for you:** This directly joins quantum field theory, exact solvability, and non-equilibrium dynamics. Its coordinated bulk–boundary drives offer concrete benchmarks for studying interacting evolution with time-dependent impurities.

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 2

### Autonomous Quantum Memories from Classical Stochastic Processes
**Nadav Drechsler, Yuval Oreg, Dorit Aharonov — arXiv:2610.05589**

The authors establish a correspondence between CSS quantum memories under Pauli noise and two associated classical stochastic memory processes. They cover both continuous-time, syndrome-based Lindblad dynamics and synchronous local quantum channels described through probabilistic cellular automata. This turns classical memory theorems into a systematic construction tool for quantum memories.

Their principal application rigorously establishes memory in the four-dimensional toric code with syndrome-based Toom corrections below a nonzero local Pauli-noise threshold. The memory error is bounded by a quantity proportional to \(m e^{-cL}\), where \(m\) counts rounds or cycles and \(L\) is linear system size, allowing exponentially long retention at fixed target error. They also lift suitable classical cellular-automaton memories to periodically driven Lindbladians. A general time-independent Lindbladian lift remains open. [v1 memory results](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05589v1)

**Background.** CSS codes separate bit-flip and phase-flip checks. Toom-type rules use asymmetric local corrections that can suppress noise without equilibrium detailed balance. Autonomous protection asks whether local dynamics can preserve encoded information without a global decoding controller.

**Why it matters for you:** The paper connects error correction to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics. The four-dimensional example establishes a principle and a proof framework; applying it to low-dimensional architectures remains a further challenge.

[arXiv 2610.05589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05589)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 3

### Two-Sided Bounds on Ground State Properties in Lattice Gauge Theories
**Eloïc Vallée, Ariel Kelman, Julius Mildenberger, Jordi Tura, Patrick Emonts — arXiv:2610.06272**

This work develops a hierarchy of semidefinite programs that supplies increasingly tight lower bounds on lattice-gauge-theory ground-state energies. Combining these with upper bounds produces certified energy intervals. The framework also propagates constraints to extended observables, including Wilson loops and mesonic strings, so certification reaches beyond a single energy estimate.

The demonstrations concern a (1+1)-dimensional Z₂ gauge theory with dynamical fermionic matter and a pure-gauge theory in (2+1) dimensions. A 129-site one-dimensional system achieves a relative interval spread of 0.04%; a 97-site two-dimensional system corresponding to a 7 × 8 lattice achieves 2.08%. These are model-specific demonstrations. Tightness depends on the chosen hierarchy level, and the examples do not establish comparable performance for general non-Abelian gauge theories.

**Background.** Variational calculations provide energy upper bounds, leaving their distance from the true ground state uncertain. Semidefinite relaxations impose positivity and consistency constraints on candidate expectation values to obtain complementary lower bounds. Wilson loops probe gauge-field correlations around closed paths; mesonic strings connect matter endpoints.

**Why it matters for you:** This combines field theory with quantum-simulation validation. Certified intervals can distinguish a quantitatively reliable simulator or variational calculation from an apparently converged but insufficiently accurate result.

[arXiv 2610.06272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06272)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 4

### How to distinguish chaos from integrability using OTOC
**Alexey Milekhin, Anders Wallberg — arXiv:2610.04197**

An out-of-time-ordered correlator can decay substantially in both chaotic and interacting integrable systems. The authors instead examine the late-time operator purity and its dependence on subsystem size. Using conserved-charge bounds, they find a distinction in the normalized quantity \(d_A^2\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{A_1A_2}^2\): at fixed subsystem fraction, the chaotic estimate depends on that fraction, whereas integrable systems retain contributions growing at least linearly with subsystem size.

Numerical spin-chain calculations support the distinction. Importantly, the claim that the diagnostic reaches its plateau in time polynomial in system size has only preliminary numerical support; the paper does not prove a general efficient observation time. The locality of conserved charges is central to the argument. [v1 analysis and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04197v1)

**Background.** Operator purity measures entanglement of an evolution operator represented as a state on doubled input and output spaces. A Mazur bound identifies persistent correlations from overlaps with conserved quantities. Integrable systems possess many such quantities, which can preserve information despite extensive scrambling.

**Why it matters for you:** This sharpens the connection between exact solvability and non-equilibrium information dynamics. It provides a more discriminating diagnostic than interpreting OTOC decay alone as evidence of chaos.

[arXiv 2610.04197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04197)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 5

### Qubit CSS codes with Universal Transversal Gates
**Arpit Dua, Adam Holmes, Michael J. Gullans, Victor V. Albert — arXiv:2610.06730**

The authors construct CSS codes with universal logical gate generators implemented as single layers of disjoint few-qubit gates, followed by error correction on the same code. Different generators use different qubit partitions. This distinction is essential: Eastin–Knill constrains transversal gates for a fixed partition, so the construction respects the theorem's assumptions.

The stored v1 abstract reports 47 constructions tolerating at least one faulty gate per generating layer, including [[37,1,7]] and [[59,1,9]] codes with check weight at most eight. A seed–kernel construction preserves a seed's non-Clifford action while increasing code distance. However, code distance alone overstates gate protection: the 37-qubit example's single-block generators have partition distance three, and two faulty native gates can cause a logical error. Its numerical noise model also optimistically assigns three-qubit gates the same failure probability as CNOTs. [v1 construction and noise model](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06730v1)

**Background.** Transversal gates limit error propagation. Here the allowed few-qubit groups change between layers, enabling universality while requiring explicit analysis of correlated faults within each group.

**Why it matters for you:** This offers a concrete alternative to magic-state-based universal computation and makes the relationship between code geometry, gate structure, and fault tolerance particularly clear.

[arXiv 2610.06730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06730)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 6

### Magic Quantum Code Surgery
**Kathleen Chang, Anasuya Lyons, Yuanjie Ren, Harald Putterman, Nathanan Tantivasadakarn, Victor V. Albert, Benjamin J. Brown, Dominic J. Williamson — arXiv:2610.03920**

This paper generalizes code surgery to fault-tolerant measurement of transversal logical Clifford operators in quantum low-density parity-check codes. Starting from a QLDPC code, the protocol adds ancillary qubits and deforms the code so that a product of stabilizers measures the desired logical operator. The intermediate construction uses Clifford stabilizers, extending beyond ordinary Pauli-stabilized surgery.

The authors prove that the deformed code retains low-density checks and preserves distance up to a constant factor independent of system size. They also establish a fault distance against data and measurement errors growing linearly with the original code distance. Applications implement logical non-Clifford operations in computational subroutines and prepare magic states. The measured operator must have the required transversal realization; the abstract does not provide a universal hardware-overhead advantage for every QLDPC architecture.

**Background.** Clifford gates map Pauli operators to Pauli operators, but measuring a logical Clifford observable can enable resources for non-Clifford computation. Code surgery performs logical measurements through temporary code deformation and additional checks. Distance preservation concerns data protection, while fault distance also accounts for faulty measurements.

**Why it matters for you:** This links error-correcting-code structure to useful logical operations. Read alongside Item 5, it presents a complementary route to universality through protected measurements.

[arXiv 2610.03920](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03920)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 7

### Free fermion models with an operator entanglement growing as $O(\sqrt{t})$
**Federico Tonetto, Jérôme Dubail — arXiv:2610.05995**

Tonetto and Dubail study operator-space entanglement in one-dimensional, number-conserving free fermions undergoing random evolution. In a Brownian Hamiltonian and a brickwork matchgate circuit, local spin operators containing Jordan–Wigner strings acquire entanglement growing as \(O(\sqrt{t})\) for all Rényi indices. The evolution operator itself exhibits the same growth law.

The analytical calculation maps the dynamics to the symmetric simple exclusion process and assumes self-averaging, supported numerically. Different randomness produces different outcomes: temporally random but spatially translation-invariant evolution also gives square-root growth, whereas quenched disorder yields \(O(\log\log t)\) growth for Hamiltonian evolution and bounded growth in the circuit example. Randomness therefore changes the scaling in ways that depend on both the operator and the evolution model.

**Background.** Operator-space entanglement treats an operator as a vector and measures its entanglement across a spatial cut. Jordan–Wigner strings make a local spin observable nonlocal in fermionic variables. The exclusion-process mapping connects quantum evolution to classical diffusion with a hard occupancy constraint.

**Why it matters for you:** This combines solvable dynamics, stochastic transport, and quantum information. It also explains why free-fermion solvability can coexist with substantial operator entanglement, affecting the usefulness of matrix-product-operator representations.

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 8

### Shallow Quantum Circuits for Geometrically Local Lindblad Dynamics
**Zheyu Shen, Yusen Wu, Xiao Yuan, Yukun Zhang — arXiv:2610.05362**

The authors give a quantum algorithm for time-independent, finite-range Lindblad evolution on a lattice of fixed spatial dimension, with bounded local strength and potentially noncommuting jump operators. For \(n\) sites, evolution time \(T\geq1\), and diamond-norm error \(\epsilon\), the circuit depth is \(O(T\operatorname{polylog}(nT/\epsilon))\), with gate count \(O(nT\operatorname{polylog}(nT/\epsilon))\).

The key device retains the environment coherently in a shared unitary dilation. Information dissipated from the system remains available, enabling reversible corrections between overlapping spatial regions. Bounds on environmental occupation allow compact simulation of the factors actually encountered. Worst-case lower bounds match the leading time and volume dependence up to polylogarithms. The construction uses nearest-neighbor gates with polylogarithmic auxiliary density, and global accuracy can require increasing depth even at fixed evolution time.

**Background.** Lindblad equations describe Markovian open-system dynamics. Spatial decomposition accelerates local Hamiltonian simulation, but dissipative channels cannot simply be inverted. A unitary dilation embeds the channel into reversible dynamics of a larger system. Diamond norm controls channel error even when the input is entangled with a reference.

**Why it matters for you:** This provides a principled resource benchmark for quantum simulation of decoherence, transport, and engineered dissipation, with physical locality built into the algorithm.

[arXiv 2610.05362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05362)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 9

### Interacting quantum criticality with U(1) symmetry breaking in 1+1 dimensions
**Mutsumi Shimomura, Kohei Kawabata — arXiv:2610.06682**

The authors present numerical evidence for an interacting critical point with transverse U(1) long-range order in finite-range spin-1 and spin-1/2 chains. Subsystem Binder scaling gives the correlation-length exponent \(\nu=0.60\pm0.02\), differing from the Gaussian value 0.5. Scaling of the lowest neutral excitation gap gives \(z=1.986\pm0.005\), close to but below the quadratic-dispersion value two.

The proposed mechanism couples phase fluctuations to longitudinal magnetization through a Berry-phase term. At criticality, softening of the conjugate magnetization permits finite phase fluctuations and transverse order. The evidence comes from numerical scaling rather than an exact solution; the small deviation of \(z\) remains a precision-sensitive result. [v1 field-theory and numerical discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06682v1)

**Background.** The classical Hohenberg–Mermin–Wagner theorem forbids continuous symmetry breaking under specified finite-temperature assumptions. Its extension to quantum ground states requires care: these systems have nonrelativistic dynamics and a Berry-phase contribution, so a simple classical correspondence does not establish a prohibition. Frustration-free ground states minimize every local Hamiltonian term simultaneously; this paper seeks a different mechanism.

**Why it matters for you:** This provides a direct connection between lattice quantum simulation, interacting field theory, and the assumptions underlying familiar symmetry-breaking restrictions.

[arXiv 2610.06682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06682)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 10

### Exact resummation of hydrodynamics for arbitrary elastic scattering integral
**Lorenzo Gavassino — arXiv:2610.05930**

Gavassino derives an exact frequency- and wave-number-dependent diffusivity for monoenergetic particles moving in three dimensions and scattering elastically from an external medium. For any rotationally invariant scattering kernel, the answer is an infinite continued fraction whose successive levels contain the relaxation rates of higher angular harmonics of the collision operator. This resums the hydrodynamic gradient expansion to all orders within the stated kinetic model.

Ordinary diffusion probes only the first angular harmonic; the resummed response retains the entire angular scattering structure. For a localized source, deviations from Fick's law decay exponentially with distance, with the decay rate fixed by the nearest complex-wave-number singularity of the inverse diffusivity. Thus even exponentially small corrections retain microscopic scattering information. Exactness here concerns elastic scattering of fixed-energy particles in an external medium, rather than general interacting-fluid hydrodynamics.

**Background.** Fick's law relates particle current to a density gradient. A gradient expansion adds corrections involving shorter spatial scales and faster dynamics. Angular harmonics resolve how scattering relaxes different directional patterns in the distribution function; continued fractions compactly retain their coupled hierarchy.

**Why it matters for you:** This is an exact bridge between kinetic theory and non-equilibrium effective descriptions, useful for understanding what microscopic information survives beyond leading diffusion.

[arXiv 2610.05930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05930)

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 11

### Polynomial-time classical algorithms for mean-field models up to the glass transition
**Alexander Schmidhuber, Alexander Zlokapa — arXiv:2610.06807**

The authors develop a rigorous quantum cavity method and use it to estimate local thermal observables of the Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model classically in polynomial time at all constant temperatures. This extends earlier sufficiently-high-temperature, quasipolynomial guarantees. Their methods also compute thermal expectations in a classical spin glass up to its phase transition. A further application learns SYK Hamiltonians from Gibbs-state data with polynomial quantum runtime and sample complexity.

The computational target is crucial: estimating local observables does not amount to preparing or classically representing the entire thermal state. Likewise, a constant temperature stays fixed as system size grows; the claim does not automatically cover temperatures tending to zero with size. [v1 results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06807v1)

**Background.** SYK consists of strongly interacting fermions with random all-to-all couplings and connects many-body physics to quantum-gravity-inspired models. Cavity methods study how a system changes when degrees of freedom are removed or added, turning effective-environment reasoning into a computational tool.

**Why it matters for you:** This broadens the quantum-algorithm discussion through a physically important model. It helps identify which thermal tasks actually require quantum resources and provides classical benchmarks for quantum simulations of strongly interacting matter.

## arXiv-2026-10-06 — Item 12

### From Equilibrium Criticality to Universal Collective Phases through Nonreciprocal Coupling of Ordered Systems
**Fabrizio Rippa, Jacopo A. Garofalo, Eugenio Lippiello — arXiv:2610.04450**

This paper studies nonreciprocal coupling between spin systems whose isolated dynamics favors order. Beyond sustained oscillations, the coupled dynamics develops multistability involving ordered and disordered states. The critical properties of the isolated systems organize the resulting behavior: tricritical structure generates hysteresis and oscillations requiring finite perturbations, while non-normal coupling enriches the multistable regimes.

The authors analyze coupled magnetization equations and derive a finite-dimensional field equation near the onset of oscillations. Their microscopic implementation adds a nonreciprocal stochastic update process to the isolated dynamics. This choice matters when interpreting universality: the detailed phase diagrams belong to the studied update rules, while the field equation addresses behavior near onset. [v1 model and analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04450v1)

**Background.** Nonreciprocity means that one subsystem's influence on another is not matched by the reverse influence. It can break detailed balance and generate persistent collective motion. A Hopf bifurcation creates oscillations when a complex-conjugate pair of stability eigenvalues crosses into instability. Non-normal dynamics can transiently amplify disturbances even when asymptotically stable.

**Why it matters for you:** This broadens your non-equilibrium interests toward active matter and stochastic collective dynamics. It shows how familiar critical phenomena can organize oscillatory and multistable behavior after reciprocal coupling is relaxed.

[arXiv 2610.04450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04450)

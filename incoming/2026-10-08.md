## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 1

### A gauge-invariant measure for lattice chiral fermions

**Nathaniel Craig — arXiv:2610.09093**

Craig proposes a nonperturbative construction of the fermion integration measure for a class of four-dimensional nonabelian chiral gauge theories containing the Standard Model. The construction compares overlap-fermion chiral projectors at successive auxiliary lattice spacings. Contributions from these comparisons are summed to obtain an exponentially local measure current, while anomaly cancellation controls convergence. The paper argues that the current satisfies the local and global consistency conditions required by Lüscher’s reconstruction of a smooth, gauge-invariant measure.

The scope needs careful separation from a complete nonperturbative continuum definition. The construction applies to admissible gauge fields, whose plaquettes obey a sufficiently small bound. The conclusion leaves reflection positivity compatible with admissibility, some phase choices between disconnected components, practical numerical implementation, and the interacting continuum theory for further work. The result is therefore a proposed solution to a central lattice-measure problem, with substantial remaining steps. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09093v1)

**Background.** Chiral gauge theories assign different gauge charges to left- and right-handed fermions. Overlap fermions accommodate lattice chirality through the Ginsparg–Wilson relation, but their allowed chiral subspace changes with the gauge field. Choosing the phase of the integration measure consistently, locally, and gauge-invariantly is then a separate challenge.

**Why it matters for you:** This directly addresses the foundations of quantum field theory. It also provides a broader connection to quantum simulation: defining a consistent regulated theory is a prerequisite for deciding what a simulator should reproduce, although this paper does not supply an efficient simulation algorithm.

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 2

### Correspondence between Asymptotic Quantum Many-Body Scars in Closed Systems and Diffusive Nambu-Goldstone Modes in Open Systems

**Masaya Kunimi, Taiki Haga, Masaya Nakagawa — arXiv:2610.09662**

The authors connect unusually slow dynamics in closed systems to diffusive relaxation in strongly dissipative open systems. They construct asymptotic rainbow scar states for broad classes of spin, fermion, and boson models. Within an enlarged scar subspace, the parent Hamiltonian has a local frustration-free decomposition. A single-mode variational construction produces orthogonal states with vanishing energy variance and characteristic entanglement scaling, together with an excitation-energy upper bound proportional to \(k^2\).

Vectorization maps rainbow scar states to maximally mixed states in fixed-charge sectors. The associated effective Lindbladian, obtained for strong local charge dephasing and a strong \(U(1)\) symmetry, shares the parent Hamiltonian’s local projectors. Uniformly bounded positive transition rates give two-sided spectral bounds and matching system-size dependence of the gaps. Identical eigenmodes require uniform rates, when the operators become proportional; a variational quadratic upper bound alone is not an exact dispersion relation. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09662v1)

**Background.** Many-body scars are atypical states that evade ordinary thermal behavior inside otherwise complex spectra. A Lindbladian generates open-system evolution, and diffusion produces decay rates proportional to \(k^2\). Vectorization represents a density operator as a vector in a doubled Hilbert space, allowing Hamiltonian and dissipative spectra to be compared. Strong-to-weak symmetry breaking describes mixed-state order compatible with ordinary symmetry-invariant averaged observables.

**Why it matters for you:** This combines non-equilibrium physics, special solvable structures, and open-system hydrodynamics. Its particularly useful connection is that coherent spectral constructions can constrain dissipative relaxation under explicit assumptions.

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 3

### A Superfluid at All Temperatures

**Sven Harder, Adar Sharon — arXiv:2610.10169**

The authors construct local, unitary \(3+1\)-dimensional conformal field theories with finitely many fields whose thermal states break a continuous global symmetry at every nonzero temperature. Finite-\(N\) corrections lift a planar circle of fixed points into isolated points; suitable gauge ranks and flavor numbers place surviving points inside its ordered region.

An infinite family reaches arbitrarily weak coupling, giving parametric perturbative control of both the fixed point and thermal order. For individual small members, the evidence is less controlled. Weakly gauging the symmetry yields superconductivity over an exponentially large temperature range. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10169v1)

**Background.** Heating usually restores symmetry, but this is not a theorem. In a conformal theory, temperature is the only thermal scale. Here, ordering one sector makes many modes in another sector lighter, increasing their entropy enough to favor the ordered state. The construction addresses limitations of earlier results relying on infinitely many fields or other idealizations.

**Why it matters for you:** This is a field-theory result about thermal symmetry breaking, with a useful connection to entropy-driven order. Its significance concerns what equilibrium QFT permits; the paper constructs relativistic theories rather than proposing a material.

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 4

### Gaussian Scrooge Ensemble from Deep Thermalization in Free-Fermions

**Angelo Russotto, Katja Klobas, Pasquale Calabrese, Bruno Bertini — arXiv:2610.10279**

This paper studies the distribution of pure subsystem states obtained by measuring the environment of an evolving free-fermion chain. The chosen local measurements preserve Gaussianity but break particle-number conservation. Although outcomes reveal some conserved-charge information at finite size, the authors argue that its influence on a fixed subsystem becomes negligible as the environment grows.

They construct a Gaussian Scrooge ensemble directly from the subsystem’s generalized Gibbs ensemble. Informally, it is a distribution of Gaussian pure states distorted and reweighted so that its average density matrix equals the appropriate generalized Gibbs state. Numerical comparisons support its description of the late-time projected ensemble at finite temperature, including information beyond that average. The emergence claim is supported by analytical arguments and numerics, rather than a general theorem for interacting integrable models or arbitrary measurements. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10279v1)

**Background.** Ordinary thermalization asks whether the reduced density matrix reaches an equilibrium form. Deep thermalization asks whether the entire distribution of measurement-conditioned pure states becomes universal. Free fermions retain many conserved quantities, requiring a generalized Gibbs ensemble rather than a temperature-only Gibbs state. Gaussian states are determined by their two-point correlations, making this a tractable setting for examining higher ensemble moments.

**Why it matters for you:** This joins integrability, non-equilibrium dynamics, and measurement. It offers a concrete way to ask how much information survives thermalization when one examines conditional states rather than only local averaged observables.

[arXiv 2610.10279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10279)

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 5

### Particle-Number-Preserving Quantum Signal Processing for Thermal Properties: Canonical-Ensemble Estimation and Grand-Canonical Reconstruction

**Taehee Ko, Sungbin Lim, Sangkook Choi — arXiv:2610.09373**

The authors assemble a quantum algorithm for estimating thermal partition functions and observables in fixed-particle-number sectors. It combines mixed Dicke-state initialization, number-preserving Trotter evolution, and generalized quantum signal processing. They then reconstruct grand-canonical quantities by classically reweighting sector-resolved estimates, allowing the same quantum data to serve scans over chemical potential.

For Hamiltonians with extensive spectral norm and exponentially large relevant sectors, they identify temperature regimes independent of system size where the asymptotic cost is lower than their classical Lanczos baseline, up to polynomial factors. Hubbard-model calculations illustrate the scaling comparison. This does not establish superiority over every classical thermal method or efficient access to arbitrary low temperatures; the quantum sampling cost remains temperature-dependent. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09373v1)

**Background.** The canonical ensemble fixes particle number, whereas the grand-canonical ensemble combines sectors through
\[
Z(\beta,\mu)=\sum_n e^{\beta\mu n}Z_n(\beta).
\]
Preserving number during simulation avoids leaving the intended sector. Quantum signal processing builds controlled functions of an evolution operator, here supplying the thermal weighting needed for estimation. Reweighting becomes useful once the required sector data have been obtained.

**Why it matters for you:** This is a quantum algorithm organized around a physical task: finite-temperature many-body observables. Its broader lesson is to treat conserved quantities and reusable measurement data as part of algorithm design, alongside circuit and sampling costs.

[arXiv 2610.09373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09373)

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 6

### Characterizing Fermionic Non-Gaussianity in the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev Model via Replica Twist Entropy

**Ning Sun, Pengfei Zhang — arXiv:2610.10123**

Sun and Zhang introduce replica twist entropy, a pure-state diagnostic formed from the expectation value of a rotation between two replicas, with adjustable angle \(\alpha\). Gaussian states possess the corresponding replica-rotation symmetry; interacting states generally lose it. At finite angle, the diagnostic captures correlations of arbitrarily high order. The paper establishes faithfulness, invariance under Gaussian unitaries, and additivity for the stated pure-state setting.

They evaluate the diagnostic for evolving thermofield-double states in the large-\(N\) Sachdev–Ye–Kitaev model. The resulting phase diagram contains spontaneous symmetry breaking in replica correlations at \(\alpha=\pi/4\), with competing saddle branches producing late-time cusps as the twist angle varies. These transitions concern the replicated diagnostic in the large-\(N\) treatment; extending the measure to mixed states remains open. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10123v1)

**Background.** Fermionic non-Gaussianity measures departure from states governed entirely by free-fermion correlations. Such states can already be entangled, so entanglement alone does not identify this departure. SYK’s random all-to-all interactions admit controlled large-\(N\) analysis of chaotic dynamics. A thermofield double is a purification of a thermal state using a second system.

**Why it matters for you:** This connects solvable many-body theory, non-equilibrium resource generation, and quantum information. Read alongside Item 4, it helps distinguish universal behavior within Gaussian dynamics from genuinely interacting correlations beyond that description.

[arXiv 2610.10123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10123)

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 7

### Numerically exact simulation of open quantum networks with strong system-bath couplings using tensor network path integrals

**Quentin W. Richter, Moritz Cygorek — arXiv:2610.10259**

Richter and Cygorek develop CTEMPO, a tensor-network contraction scheme for strong-coupling, non-Markovian dynamics. Relative to approaches that first build a bath process tensor, it incorporates system propagators earlier, shortening the intermediate matrix-product representation and improving compressibility. A comb geometry then connects local bath-history tensors to the many-body system’s tensor-network backbone.

Spin-boson benchmarks show orders-of-magnitude computational improvements over established methods in the studied regimes. The network method also handles structured environments and is applied to collective emission from up to twenty emitters with individual phonon baths. Here, “numerically exact” means a systematically controlled numerical treatment: time discretization and tensor compression still introduce errors, and the examples do not imply uniformly efficient simulation of arbitrary networks. The paper explicitly compares compression errors in its benchmarks. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10259v1)

**Background.** A non-Markovian bath remembers earlier system evolution, so a time-local weak-coupling master equation can miss important dynamics. Influence functionals encode that memory as interactions between different times. Tensor networks compress the resulting histories, but strong coupling can inflate bond dimensions. Changing contraction order can therefore determine whether an otherwise exact formalism is computationally usable.

**Why it matters for you:** This supplies a practical numerical connection between open-system theory and non-equilibrium many-body physics. The relevant contribution is the general method for strong bath coupling and memory, including benchmarks against which simpler dynamical approximations can be tested.

[arXiv 2610.10259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10259)

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 8

### Building codes with transversal CCZ using projective geometry and SAT solvers

**Bohan Lu, Kenneth R. Brown — arXiv:2610.09341**

The authors construct CSS codes encoding three logical qubits, with logical CCZ implemented by transversal physical \(T/T^\dagger\) gates and code distance at least three. Projective geometry supplies \(X\)-stabilizers whose weights are divisible by eight and guarantees \(d_Z\geq3\). A satisfiability solver then finds compatible logical \(X\) operators. The construction yields codes at thirteen block lengths between 48 and 496, with \(Z\)-distance three or four, including an explicit \([[48,3,3]]\) example.

They also prove a lower bound: no CSS code with three logical qubits, \(Z\)-distance at least three, and a quasi-transversal CCZ—transversal \(T\) followed by a diagonal Clifford correction—exists below 39 physical qubits. An existing 47-qubit construction leaves lengths 39 through 46 unresolved. The new family thus establishes constructions and a restricted lower bound, without closing the minimum-size question. [v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09341v1)

**Background.** CSS codes separate bit- and phase-error checks. Transversal gates act independently on physical qubits, limiting how a fault spreads during the gate. CCZ applies a minus sign to the three-qubit state \(|111\rangle\) and supplies a non-Clifford operation needed beyond stabilizer processing. The divisibility conditions constrain the phases induced by physical \(T\) rotations.

**Why it matters for you:** This directly concerns fault-tolerant non-Clifford computation. The useful broader connection is between code geometry, constructive search, and rigorous resource bounds, with a concrete gate mechanism rather than an abstract algorithmic complexity result.

[arXiv 2610.09341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09341)

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 9

### Vortex screening and the fate of the BKT transition with long-range couplings

**Guido Giachetti — arXiv:2610.10171**

Giachetti studies the two-dimensional XY model with interactions decaying as \(r^{-2-\sigma}\). A real-space renormalization-group treatment retains the full many-body vortex interaction, showing that screening by small vortex–antivortex pairs preserves its Gaussian-average representation at the retained leading order. Adding spin-wave effects perturbatively gives a local stability criterion for the quasi-long-range-ordered fixed line.

A stable interval requires \(\sigma>7/4\). For \(\sigma<7/4\), the retained flow contains a finite-coupling fixed point whose relevant eigenvalue predicts \(\nu^{-1}\sim\sqrt{7/4-\sigma}\) near its merger with the Gaussian line. These conclusions have different scopes: the first concerns local stability near quasi-long-range order; the second is a prediction of truncated flows. Finite-coupling trajectories may bypass the stable basin, so the criterion alone does not determine every microscopic model’s complete phase diagram. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10171v1)

**Background.** The BKT phase has correlations that decay algebraically, rather than true long-range order. Its transition involves vortex unbinding. Long-range interactions compete with these topological defects by favoring stronger order. In the perturbative stability analysis, irrelevance of the long-range coupling requires \(\eta>2-\sigma\), while vortex stability requires \(\eta<1/4\); an open interval therefore needs \(\sigma>7/4\).

**Why it matters for you:** This connects field-theory reasoning, topological defects, and long-range statistical mechanics. It is also relevant to interpreting programmable simulators whose interactions have algebraic tails, where short-range universality assumptions need scrutiny.

### Root systems and Minimal RG flows to c=1 CFTs

**Changrim Ahn, Sara Kostadinovic, Francesco Ravanini — arXiv:2610.08938**

The authors extend Dorey’s geometric construction of massive integrable scattering matrices to massless scattering. Root-system geometry generates amplitudes satisfying bootstrap consistency equations. From these amplitudes they derive thermodynamic Bethe ansatz equations for minimal renormalization-group flows ending at the \(D_n\) coset theories, which have central charge one and compact-boson radius \(\sqrt{n/2}\).

The analysis identifies their ultraviolet completions and provides scattering-based support for flows previously suggested by guessed TBA systems. The scope is a special family of integrable two-dimensional theories, whose additional conservation laws make exact scattering possible. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08938v1)

**Background.** A conformal field theory describes a scale-invariant endpoint of RG evolution. Integrable scattering factorizes many-particle processes into consistent two-particle amplitudes. The thermodynamic Bethe ansatz turns those amplitudes into equations for thermodynamic quantities and scale-dependent effective central charges. Lie-algebra root systems organize the particle species and scattering relations.

**Why it matters for you:** This provides an exact connection between microscopic scattering and macroscopic RG flow. It fits your interests in QFT and solvable models, while offering analytical benchmarks for critical behavior beyond a single fixed point.

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 11

### Efficient Estimation of Logical Sensitivities Through Fault-Counting

**Winston Fu, J. Wilson Staples, Jeff D. Thompson — arXiv:2610.10531**

This paper estimates how each physical noise parameter affects a quantum error-correcting circuit’s logical failure probability. Instead of repeating simulations at perturbed error rates, the authors extract all derivatives \(\nu_i=\partial p_L/\partial p_i\) from one Monte Carlo dataset at a single noise configuration. The method uses the sampled fault configurations and the derivative of their log-probability—a score-function estimator.

Circuit-level surface-code benchmarks agree with finite differences while requiring one to two orders of magnitude fewer shots for comparable variance. Applications include error budgets, individual-qubit sensitivities, effective-distance estimates, and Newton searches for threshold contours in multidimensional noise models. The method relies on access to underlying simulated faults; ordinary hardware syndrome records do not directly provide that information. Results also depend on the specified noise and decoding model. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10531v1)

**Background.** A logical error rate combines several physical mechanisms, such as faulty gates, measurements, and idle evolution. For small parameter changes,
\[
\delta p_L\approx\sum_i\nu_i\,\delta p_i.
\]
Differentiating the probability distribution of faults lets one estimate these responses without differentiating the discrete failure outcome itself. Thresholds then become surfaces in noise-parameter space rather than a single universal number.

**Why it matters for you:** This adds a practical diagnostic to the code-construction perspective of Item 8. It connects fault tolerance to statistical response estimation and helps identify which physical improvements would most effectively reduce logical failures within a chosen model.

[arXiv 2610.10531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10531)

## arXiv-2026-10-08 — Item 12

### Geometric heat pumping on a quantum processor

**Gerónimo J. Caselli, Luis O. Manuel, Liliana Arrachea, Pablo S. Cornaglia — arXiv:2610.09062**

The authors demonstrate heat pumping between equal-temperature emulated reservoirs on a superconducting quantum processor. A periodically driven working qubit repeatedly interacts with two ancilla qubits prepared in thermal states. Energy measurements before and after interactions reconstruct the heat exchanged with each reservoir. Reversing the driving contour isolates a contribution associated with pumping.

The measured dependence on driving period and contour agrees with exact collision-model simulations without fitted parameters. In the slow-driving regime, the response approaches the geometric limit of the corresponding Lindblad dynamics. The demonstration uses prepared ancillas to emulate reservoirs; contour-only behavior is the adiabatic limit, while finite-period effects remain relevant. This is a test of a dynamical principle, with external driving providing the control needed to transport energy at equal temperature. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09062v1)

**Background.** Adiabatic pumping produces transport by cycling parameters even without a static bias. Its geometric component depends on the path through parameter space, analogously to a Berry phase, and changes sign when that path is reversed. Collision models realize open-system evolution through repeated interactions with fresh environmental units, making energy accounting accessible through local measurements.

**Why it matters for you:** This experiment qualifies through its demonstration of a theoretical principle linking geometry, driven open systems, and quantum thermodynamics. It also shows how a small quantum processor can probe non-equilibrium response rather than only reproduce closed-system Hamiltonian evolution.

### Kardar-Parisi-Zhang superdiffusion in chaotic quantum circuits
**Rustem Sharipov, Urban Duh, Sun Woo P. Kim, Friedrich Hübner — arXiv:2610.07143**

This work constructs chaotic quantum circuits whose conserved-charge transport belongs to the full Kardar–Parisi–Zhang universality class, with dynamical exponent $z=3/2$. Nonlinear fluctuating hydrodynamics predicts the propagation speed, broadening scale, and correlation profile from microscopic equilibrium data. Tensor-network calculations for a qutrit circuit reproduce these predictions without fitted transport parameters.

A qubit construction also switches between diffusion and superdiffusion as the chemical potential changes. Its persistent coherent quasiparticles slow the approach to the asymptotic KPZ profile. The result establishes examples beyond integrable systems; it does not imply that arbitrary chaotic circuits exhibit KPZ transport.

**Background.** Diffusion spreads a conserved fluctuation over a width proportional to $t^{1/2}$; KPZ broadening scales as $t^{2/3}$. A nonlinear dependence of the equilibrium current on density can couple hydrodynamic fluctuations and change their long-time scaling.

**Why it matters for you:** It connects non-equilibrium quantum dynamics with stochastic universality and helps separate anomalous transport from integrability. The microscopic predictions also provide concrete benchmarks for quantum simulation.

## arXiv-2026-10-07 — Item 2

### Protecting bosonic codes from ancilla-induced errors with continuous-variable flags
**Thomas Décultot, Ronan Gautier, Mazyar Mirrahimi — arXiv:2610.07139**

An ancilla qubit can decay during an oscillator gate and propagate an error whose magnitude depends continuously on the decay time. The proposed remedy couples an auxiliary oscillator to that ancilla, recording information about the error in phase space. Heterodyne measurement and feedback then compensate it, up to a code stabilizer.

The authors construct conditional-rotation protection to all orders in ancilla decay using one flag. Conditional displacements receive first-order protection with one flag and all-orders protection with two. Circuit-QED simulations predict over a thousandfold reduction in logical errors for a four-component cat-code parity measurement, and finite-energy GKP lifetimes approaching the ideal-ancilla limit. These are modeled performance estimates.

**Background.** Bosonic codes store a logical qubit in oscillator states. Conventional flags identify dangerous fault events; an oscillator flag can additionally estimate a continuous displacement or rotation that a binary flag cannot resolve.

**Why it matters for you:** The paper addresses a fault-propagation mechanism central to bosonic error correction and develops an architecture relevant to hybrid oscillator–qubit simulation.

### Spontaneous Hawking Radiation from Dynamical Black-Hole Formation in Quantum Spin Systems
**Shunichiro Kinoshita, Keiju Murata, Daisuke Yamamoto, Ryosuke Yoshii — arXiv:2610.07794**

The authors model horizon formation using local quantum spins, starting from a ground state representing the early-time vacuum. Radiation emerges from the changing geometry without preparing initial excitations. They calculate the renormalized energy density in the corresponding continuum field theory and identify a local spin observable reproducing it, including its surface-gravity dependence.

The agreement lasts for a finite interval growing logarithmically with system size, after which lattice effects become relevant. The measured quantity represents an energy-weighted integral of the Hawking spectrum, so the comparison establishes a specific observable rather than a reconstruction of every emitted mode.

**Background.** In a time-dependent spacetime, early and late definitions of particles can differ. Bogoliubov mixing relates their mode bases and produces particles from the initial vacuum. Surface gravity sets the characteristic Hawking temperature.

**Why it matters for you:** This is a direct bridge between quantum field theory and many-body simulation, with a local observable and an explicit account of how the lattice limits continuum behavior.

### Chiral Parafermions in Floquet $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Topological Order
**Melissa Will, Harald Schmid, Jan Egger, Adam Gammon-Smith, Michael Knap, Frank Pollmann — arXiv:2610.07156**

A three-step periodic drive realizes $\mathbb{Z}_N$ Floquet topological order in a generalized Kitaev honeycomb model built from clock variables. The emergent description combines constrained parafermions with conserved gauge fields. Boundary correlations reveal directional parafermion propagation, while a bulk loop observable probes the drive's conversion of anyon types.

The authors characterize fractional information transport using the radical chiral unitary index and examine exchange statistics through dynamics. At a special Clifford point, stabilizer methods make the model efficiently simulable. Matrix-product-state calculations away from that point retain the chiral edge response over accessible short times. This supports persistence beyond the solvable setting, while leaving generic long-time stability unresolved.

**Background.** Clock variables generalize two-state spins to $N$ states; parafermions similarly extend Majorana algebra. Floquet topological order can encode a repeating transformation of quasiparticles and directional boundary information flow, properties that a static equilibrium phase need not possess. The chiral unitary index measures net information transported along an edge during a drive cycle.

**Why it matters for you:** The model joins non-equilibrium dynamics, gauge structure, and exactly tractable circuit limits. It also supplies a concrete target for qudit quantum simulation beyond ordinary spin-$1/2$ systems.

### A Single Conformal Coordinate Decodes Outcome-Resolved Measurement-Induced Entanglement
**Reyhaneh Khasseh, M. A. Rajabpour — arXiv:2610.07995**

The authors find that the universal conditional Rényi entropy between unmeasured regions of critical chains can be predicted asymptotically from one compact coordinate extracted from the measurement record. Compact-boson field theory explains why spatially varying boundary components cancel from the normalized entropy, leaving a relative zero mode.

Exact XX-chain identities and numerical calculations test this description. Interacting XXZ calculations use the same microscopic coordinate without retuning it, while the continuum prediction retains the interaction-dependent Luttinger coupling. The [v1 analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07995v1) explicitly leaves microscopic XXZ matching open. The compression concerns this universal entropy, rather than the entire conditioned state or all finite-size details.

**Background.** Rényi entropies characterize reduced-state spectra. A compact boson is a periodic scalar field describing Luttinger liquids; its zero mode captures an average field coordinate rather than spatial oscillations.

**Why it matters for you:** It connects measurement-conditioned quantum information with continuum field theory and exact lattice calculations, showing how exponentially many outcomes can organize around a simple universal variable.

### Conformal invariance of the six-vertex model's height function
**Emile Averous, Hugo Duminil-Copin, Tiancheng He, Piet Lammers, Ioan Manolescu — arXiv:2610.07454**

This paper proves that six-vertex height fluctuations in simply connected domains with specified piecewise constant boundary data converge to a Gaussian free field with Dirichlet boundary conditions. This establishes conformal invariance of the continuum limit for a continuous range of model parameters.

The argument transfers known full-plane convergence into bounded domains using reflection positivity and qualitative estimates. Its established parameter coverage includes a neighborhood of $\Delta=0$ and $(-1,-1/2]$, rather than the entire critical regime. The [v1 theorem statements](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07454v1) formulate the result using the available full-plane convergence input and explicit boundary assumptions.

**Background.** The six-vertex model assigns arrows obeying a local ice rule; these configurations define a height field. The Gaussian free field is a massless scalar continuum theory. Conformal invariance describes how its correlations transform under angle-preserving changes of geometry.

**Why it matters for you:** It makes a foundational connection between an integrable lattice model and continuum field theory rigorous, including boundary effects that bulk solvability alone does not settle.

### Algebraic Tensor Network Renormalization and Holographic duality
**Chenqi Meng, Tian Lan, Zhengcheng Gu — arXiv:2610.08787**

The authors develop $\mathcal{R}$-TNR, a tensor-network renormalization method that incorporates fusion-category constraints into local tensor spaces and preserves them during coarse graining. In Ising, tricritical Ising, and three-state Potts examples, broad families of allowed initial tensors approach the corresponding gapless fixed points.

The method separates conformal spectra by topological sector and extracts scaling dimensions, conformal spins, and magnitudes of structure constants. Its topological bootstrap uses finite categorical and geometric input to recover infrared conformal data. The reported stability occurs within the constrained construction; arbitrary symmetry-breaking perturbations need not preserve criticality. The [v1 conclusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08787v1) also discusses studying relevant perturbations through their renormalization flows.

**Background.** Tensor-network renormalization replaces microscopic degrees of freedom with effective tensors at larger scales. Fusion categories encode consistent rules for combining generalized symmetry defects or topological charges. Preserving those rules can restrict which perturbations the coarse-grained theory permits.

**Why it matters for you:** It connects field theory, generalized symmetry, and computational many-body methods. The focus is on recovering physical conformal observables, providing a broader connection between topological data and critical dynamics.

### Cyclic triorthogonal codes in prime dimension
**Shiroman Prakash — arXiv:2610.08012**

This work constructs cyclic qudit codes supporting a transversal diagonal gate that implements a logical non-Clifford operation. For lengths coprime to the prime local dimension, a spectral-support sumset condition reduces the triorthogonality search to a tractable enumeration problem.

Examples include $[[19,1,3]]_3$ and $[[7,1,3]]_7$, reported to improve distillation thresholds and overhead exponents relative to the shortest Reed–Muller and Reed–Solomon comparators. The latter belongs to the family $[[p,1,2\lfloor p/6\rfloor+1]]_p$. These comparisons concern particular constructions and distillation metrics; they do not establish a universally optimal hardware architecture.

**Background.** A $[[n,k,d]]_p$ code stores $k$ logical qudits in $n$ physical qudits with distance $d$. Transversal gates act separately on physical constituents, limiting error propagation. Non-Clifford operations supply the resource needed to go beyond stabilizer computation, often through magic-state distillation.

**Why it matters for you:** It adds a concrete fault-tolerance advance for qudits, complementing the batch's qudit simulation models and connecting algebraic code structure to computational resource costs.

### QCD_2: 't Hooft equation vs. relativistic Schroedinger equation
**Andrei Smilga — arXiv:2610.08673**

Smilga compares two descriptions of meson masses in two-dimensional QCD with a fundamental quark: the field-theoretic 't Hooft integral equation and the simpler relativistic Hamiltonian

$$H=2\sqrt{p^2+m^2}+\sigma|x|,\qquad \sigma=g^2N/4.$$

The theories agree at leading semiclassical order but have different subleading WKB corrections. Those differences matter substantially for the ground state. By excitation index $n=3$, the relative discrepancy is at most approximately $2.5\%$ and decreases rapidly thereafter, according to the v1 comparison.

**Background.** The 't Hooft limit takes $N\to\infty$ with $g^2N$ fixed, simplifying gauge dynamics while retaining a tower of confined mesons. A linear potential captures string confinement, but reproducing its leading semiclassical spectrum does not guarantee the correct low-energy quantum corrections. WKB organizes a spectrum around classical motion and successive quantum corrections.

**Why it matters for you:** This is a compact, controlled field-theory benchmark for judging effective Hamiltonians. It connects confinement with tractable spectral calculations and clarifies when an intuitive potential model captures the relevant physics—and when its apparent agreement hides important corrections.

### Fault-tolerant resource estimation for ground-state preparation via Lindblad simulation
**Marius Bothe, Patrick Schoepf, Nick S. Blunt — arXiv:2610.08667**

The authors evaluate a single-ancilla Lindblad algorithm for preparing low-energy Hubbard states. They derive error bounds with explicit prefactors and compare them with circuit-level simulations, whose empirically chosen parameters reduce estimated costs by orders of magnitude.

Even with those choices, the estimate is $7.7\times10^8$ T gates for **one unit of evolution** targeting the low-energy subspace of a 36-site model. Total preparation also depends on convergence time. Energy-transition filtering dominates the cost. The [v1 analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08667v1) separates rigorous bounds from empirical extrapolations and finds that dissipative contractivity generally does not protect the implementation against device noise.

**Background.** Engineered Lindblad jumps can favor energy-lowering transitions. Implementing the required spectral filter involves Hamiltonian evolution over a range of times; finer energy resolution increases that effort. T-gate counts track an expensive resource in fault-tolerant computation.

**Why it matters for you:** It grounds an appealing open-system quantum algorithm in concrete simulation costs and identifies filtering, convergence, and hardware errors as separate challenges.

**Andrew Lucas — arXiv:2610.07693**

Lucas constructs a nearest-neighbor classical chain with infinite on-site state space $\mathbb{Z}^2$ and a helical symmetry combining flavor transformations with translation. For every finite positive inverse temperature, the model has uncountably many infinite-volume Gibbs states, supporting an interpretation as spontaneous symmetry breaking.

A finite periodic regularization has a unique Gibbs state whose probability concentrates in separated symmetry-related clusters. This provides evidence beyond the formal existence of multiple infinite-volume states. The construction relies on unusual assumptions: unbounded local states and symmetry intertwined with translation. A continuous-state variant breaks a noncompact symmetry, outside the usual Mermin–Wagner setting, as explained in the [v1 discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.07693v1).

**Background.** Infinite-volume Gibbs states satisfy local equilibrium consistency conditions, commonly called DLR conditions. Multiple such states can label different thermodynamic sectors. Familiar prohibitions on one-dimensional ordering depend on additional assumptions beyond short-range interactions alone.

**Why it matters for you:** This broadens the selection through a precise statistical-mechanics example that exposes the assumptions behind symmetry-breaking lore and connects internal symmetry with spatial structure.

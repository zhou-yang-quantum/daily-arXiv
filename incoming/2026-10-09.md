### Analog quantum simulation of $φ^4$ field theory with a superconducting transmission line

**Ilan T. Rosen, Neill C. Warrington, Max Hays, Christopher McNally, Joshua Lin, Stephen Sorokanich, Julian Bender — arXiv:2610.11071**

The authors propose an analog simulator of interacting scalar field theory in one spatial dimension using a transmission line assembled from fluxonium-like circuit elements. Continuous, unbounded circuit-phase variables represent the scalar field, avoiding a finite local Hilbert-space truncation. Anharmonic circuit potentials supply the interaction. Lattice Monte Carlo comparisons of low-energy spectra support the correspondence between the circuit and lattice $φ^4$ theory.

Two operating regimes target different scattering problems. A single-well potential supports collisions between particle wave packets; a double-well potential supports symmetry-broken vacua and kink–antikink collisions. The proposal includes preparation and measurement protocols for spatially resolved real-time observations. Its demonstrated correspondence concerns low-energy spectra, and the collision protocols remain proposals: unbounded local variables alone do not establish exact continuum dynamics at arbitrary energies. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11071v1)

**Background.** A $φ^4$ interaction is a basic setting for studying interacting fields and spontaneous symmetry breaking. A kink interpolates between distinct vacua across space. Real-time scattering is difficult to obtain from conventional imaginary-time Monte Carlo, motivating a simulator that directly evolves the field.

**Why it matters for you:** This directly combines quantum field theory, quantum simulation, and non-equilibrium dynamics. Its treatment of both elementary excitations and solitons makes it a useful platform proposal for comparing perturbative particle descriptions with nonlinear collective dynamics.

[arXiv 2610.11071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11071)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 2

### Single-shot magic state factories for quantum LDPC codes

**Varun Menon, Rohan Mehta, Andi Gu, Andrei C. Diaconu, Daniel Bochen Tan, Xiao Xiao, Michael J. Gullans, Qian Xu, Hengyun Zhou, Mikhail D. Lukin, J. Pablo Bonilla Ataides — arXiv:2610.10731**

This work combines two code families with complementary strengths. Tricycle codes supply transversal non-Clifford operations; four-dimensional balanced-product quadcycle codes supply stronger storage protection and single-shot error correction. A shallow teleportation step transfers postselected logical magic into the receiving code, retaining the advantage of encoding several logical qubits per block.

At physical error rate $10^{-3}$ under the specified circuit-level noise model, the authors report CCZ-state error rates reaching approximately $10^{-9}$ and space-time costs 10–40 times below comparison methods. They also provide a neutral-atom implementation using reconfigurable transport. These are modeled resource estimates. The comparison normalizes competing T-state factories by their CCZ yield and excludes decoder latency and additional T-to-CCZ conversion-circuit costs. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10731v1)

**Background.** Clifford operations alone do not provide universal quantum computation. Magic states enable the missing non-Clifford operations, often at substantial cost. Single-shot correction uses redundancy in a constant number of syndrome-measurement rounds, rather than requiring repetitions that grow with code distance. “Four-dimensional” describes the code construction, rather than four physical spatial dimensions.

**Why it matters for you:** It addresses a central fault-tolerance bottleneck with consequences for quantum algorithms and field-theory simulation. The conceptual contribution is the division of labor between codes optimized for generating a resource and codes optimized for preserving it.

[arXiv 2610.10731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10731)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 3

### Markov length can diverge in systems whose universal physics is spatially Markovian

**Yu-Hsueh Chen, Tarun Grover — arXiv:2610.11235**

The paper identifies a tension between renormalization-group universality and information-theoretic definitions of mixed-state phases. A critical Gibbs state of a finite-range classical Hamiltonian has vanishing conditional mutual information beyond the interaction range. An RG-irrelevant perturbation that breaks detailed balance can nevertheless generate algebraically decaying conditional correlations, giving an infinite Markov length at any fixed lattice cutoff.

The crucial distinction is that these tails disappear in the continuum limit: their amplitudes are suppressed by positive powers of the cutoff. Solvable examples establish the mechanism, while the analogous behavior at an Ising-symmetric Toom cellular-automaton critical point is a prediction. Under the paper’s scaling assumptions, a one-dimensional criterion relates decay faster than inverse-square buffer length to spatially Markovian continuum physics. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11235v1)

**Background.** Conditional mutual information, $I(A:C|B)=S(AB)+S(BC)-S(B)-S(ABC)$, measures correlations remaining between separated regions after conditioning on the intervening region. Its decay controls approximate recovery and local reversibility. Spatial Markov behavior concerns conditional dependence across regions; it differs from memorylessness in time.

**Why it matters for you:** This connects non-equilibrium physics, field-theoretic scaling, and quantum information. It shows why a divergent information-theoretic length need not imply new continuum universal physics, while still obstructing local reversibility on the microscopic lattice.

[arXiv 2610.11235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11235)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 4

### Constructing Large and Structured Decoherence-Free Subspaces in Hybrid Quantum Systems

**Catalin-Mihai Halati, Tony Jin, Roderich Moessner — arXiv:2610.12348**

The authors develop a construction in which quantum matter couples to a lossy bosonic mode, and dissipation selects the null space of a matter operator. Lie-algebra representation theory provides a way to design large, structured null spaces. Their explicit example couples a lossy cavity to directed tunneling of one-dimensional particles; an $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})$ structure organizes an exponentially large steady-state manifold.

Analytical and numerical results reveal long-range kinetic correlations, volume-law entanglement, and a symmetry-constrained Liouvillian skin effect. Time-dependent matrix-product-state simulations show how nontrivial properties emerge from generic initial states. The protected manifold can possess symmetries absent from the full dissipative dynamics. Protection is tied to the engineered coupling and constraints; the result does not establish robustness against arbitrary additional noise. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12348v1)

**Background.** A decoherence-free subspace contains states that the relevant environmental coupling cannot distinguish or disturb. Here its size and internal structure arise from an operator’s null space. A Liouvillian governs density-matrix evolution, and a skin effect describes boundary accumulation associated with nonreciprocal dynamics.

**Why it matters for you:** This combines non-equilibrium many-body physics, algebraically tractable models, and quantum-information protection. The broader lesson is that engineered loss can impose useful many-body constraints and sustain complex correlations, suggesting a bridge between dissipative state preparation and passive information storage.

[arXiv 2610.12348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12348)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 5

### Quantum annealing of the random transverse-field Ising model in one, two, and three dimensions

**István A. Kovács, Neil Shah, Róbert Juhász — arXiv:2610.11411**

This study analyzes defect production during quantum annealing through strongly disordered Ising criticality. Strong-disorder renormalization treats the evolution as successive aggregation of strongly coupled spin clusters. Combining this description with adiabaticity conditions gives a scaling theory for elementary events where excitations can form.

Extensive calculations in one, two, and three dimensions support a logarithmically slow decrease of defect density with annealing time, governed by dimension-dependent exponents of the infinite-disorder fixed point. One-dimensional free-fermion calculations provide an independent check. Maps of potential defect events in excitation-energy versus control-parameter space suggest a route toward improved schedules, although the paper does not establish a universally optimal annealing protocol. Higher-dimensional evidence relies on the strong-disorder approach. [v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11411v1)

**Background.** Near an ordinary quantum critical point, increasing annealing time often reduces defects through a power-law scaling relation. Infinite-disorder criticality instead has extremely broad energy and time scales, producing activated scaling and logarithmically slow improvement. Rare, weakly connected clusters create difficult adiabatic bottlenecks.

**Why it matters for you:** It connects quantum algorithms to physical dynamics without centering on computational complexity. The results clarify when disorder changes the basic economics of slow state preparation, and the one-dimensional exact treatment supplies a useful benchmark for approximate higher-dimensional reasoning.

[arXiv 2610.11411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11411)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 6

### Generalised Gibbs Ensembles from the ODE/IM Correspondence: the Lee--Yang Model

**Sujay K. Ashok, Max Downing, Faisal Karimi, Adarsh Sudhakar, Roberto Tateo — arXiv:2610.10716**

The authors extend the correspondence between ordinary differential equations and integrable models to generalized Gibbs ensembles. In the chiral Lee–Yang conformal field theory, they encode conserved-charge chemical potentials through a deformation of a cubic oscillator. Matching its leading semiclassical period to the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz driving term fixes the deformation; subleading periods then predict deformed charge eigenvalues.

For the spin-5 ensemble, they work out the ground state and the identity primary. The latter requires an additional apparent singularity fixed by a monodromy condition. Charge eigenvalues agree with independent conformal-field-theory calculations at the orders checked, and Borel-resummed ground-state energies agree with numerical Bethe-ansatz solutions over a broad chemical-potential range. The detailed tests concern these selected states in a nonunitary model; extensions to other integrable theories require further application. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10716v1)

**Background.** Integrable systems retain many conserved quantities, motivating ensembles of the form $\rho\propto\exp[-\sum_s\mu_sQ_s]$. The ODE/IM correspondence translates quantum spectral information into differential-equation data. WKB periods give semiclassical expansions, while Borel resummation extracts information beyond a straightforward truncated series.

**Why it matters for you:** It provides a concrete connection between exactly solvable field theory and generalized statistical mechanics. Its value is a calculational framework for conserved-charge deformations, with several independent checks rather than a title-level appeal to integrability.

[arXiv 2610.10716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10716)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 7

### Measure Now, Mitigate Later: Virtual Error Cancellation for Logical Quantum Circuits

**Oles Shtanko, Saswata Roy, Shashwat Kumar, Zlatko Minev — arXiv:2610.12400**

Virtual error cancellation uses stored syndrome records to mitigate residual errors after a universal logical circuit has finished. A fast decoder operates during execution; a more expensive decoder later reanalyzes the records. Their agreement and disagreement divide shots into sectors that receive signed weights, allowing cancellation of logical bias through classical post-processing.

Distance-7 rotated-surface-code simulations show suppression exceeding three orders of magnitude relative to the decoded logical error rate. The method avoids separate prior calibration experiments and circuit modifications, but still learns error-model parameters from the syndromes. Exact cancellation depends on correct noise parameters, and signed reweighting increases statistical uncertainty. This improves computational estimates after execution; it does not restore each individual corrupted quantum state. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12400v1)

**Background.** Error correction leaves occasional logical failures when a finite code cannot identify the true error. Error mitigation instead reduces bias in estimated observables, typically by spending more samples. Syndrome records contain information beyond the correction chosen in real time, especially when that decoder faces strict latency constraints.

**Why it matters for you:** It connects practical quantum error correction with the observable-estimation tasks needed in quantum simulation. The useful broader idea is to preserve measurement records as a computational resource: later classical analysis can extract additional value from an already completed quantum experiment.

[arXiv 2610.12400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12400)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 8

### Hamiltonian framework for Chiral Gauge Theories on a Disk Boundary

**Srimoyee Sen — arXiv:2610.11035**

This paper addresses a specific obstruction to constructing a Hamiltonian for a disk-boundary formulation of chiral gauge theories. The existing Euclidean construction places chiral fermions on a higher-dimensional disk’s boundary and extends gauge fields into its interior using bulk equations of motion. The proposed replacement makes that extension compatible with both Hamiltonian and Euclidean descriptions.

For Abelian gauge fields, the author obtains exact interior-field expressions as functionals of boundary fields. Euclidean analysis argues that the extension can preserve nonperturbative features, including topological charge and associated fermion zero modes. The discussion also addresses strong CP behavior in the proposed Standard Model construction. The concrete advance is a route past the extension bottleneck; the paper does not supply a completed quantum simulation of the full non-Abelian Standard Model. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11035v1)

**Background.** Chiral gauge theories couple differently to left- and right-handed fermions. Lattice regularization must confront fermion-doubling constraints, while conventional Euclidean calculations can face sign problems. A Hamiltonian formulation is therefore valuable for real-time methods and quantum simulation, even after a Euclidean regulator has been proposed.

**Why it matters for you:** This connects foundational quantum field theory to simulator design at the level of the regulator itself. It highlights that a formal path-integral construction must also yield usable dynamical variables before becoming a quantum-computing target.

[arXiv 2610.11035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11035)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 9

### Quantum non-Markovian response spectra

**Aaron Sander, John F. Kam, Gauthameshwar S., Robert Wille, Kavan Modi — arXiv:2610.10684**

The authors introduce a response matrix that probes environmental memory using controlled histories and subsequent observations of the accessible system. Its entries measure how future observations depend on past interventions, including specified resets. The matrix spectrum characterizes the structure and persistence of that dependence without directly measuring the environment.

The framework is developed theoretically and studied in an interacting spin process; a two-qubit benchmark certifies quantum memory. Suitable combinations of response entries exclude specified classical memory models. This qualification matters: the witnesses test defined alternatives, while the response spectra depend on the interventions and resets used. Independent history–future queries permit parallel numerical evaluation, but the work does not claim complete environmental reconstruction at universally inexpensive cost. [v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10684v1)

**Background.** Resetting a qubit need not reset an environment that has stored information about earlier operations. That memory can feed back later, producing temporal correlations and correlations between processor jobs. Full multi-time process tomography becomes expensive because the number of possible histories grows rapidly.

**Why it matters for you:** It links open-system non-equilibrium physics to correlated noise in quantum computation. Alongside Item 3, it also offers a useful distinction: this paper studies temporal environmental memory, whereas conditional mutual information there diagnoses spatial dependence and recoverability.

[arXiv 2610.10684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10684)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 10

### Large Charge Jets in $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM Energy Correlators

**Alba Grassi, Cristoforo Iossa, Luigi Tizzano, Alexander Zhiboedov — arXiv:2610.10707**

The authors compute an energy–energy correlator in a heavy canonical-operator state of $\mathcal{N}=4$ $SU(N)$ super Yang–Mills theory. At fixed $N$, the large-charge limit retains arbitrary effective coupling $\lambda\sim\Delta_Hg_{\rm YM}^2$. Starting from a known finite-coupling four-point function, they perform detector and Fourier transforms and analyze an infinite operator-product-expansion sum.

The angular dependence exhibits several regimes: vacuum-dominated behavior at very small separation, a medium-induced crossover, a jet regime scaling as $1/(\lambda z^3)$, and an approximately angle-independent plateau. Here $z=(1-\cos\theta)/2$, with the jet window extending parametrically between $\lambda^{-1}$ and $\lambda^{-1/3}$. The result concerns a special heavy-state, large-charge limit of a supersymmetric conformal theory; its physical interpretation does not make it a direct QCD prediction. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10707v1)

**Background.** Energy correlators describe how energy deposits at different detector directions are correlated. Large quantum numbers can keep interaction effects finite even as the microscopic coupling decreases, creating controlled limits that access effective strong coupling.

**Why it matters for you:** This is a field-theory paper organized around an observable and distinct physical regimes. It broadens the exact-model coverage beyond one-dimensional integrability and illustrates how solvable correlation functions can reveal nontrivial angular energy transport.

[arXiv 2610.10707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10707)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 11

### Short-Range Vector Spin Glasses: Parisi Hierarchies and Real-Space Ultrametricity

**Akash Vijay, Jaewon Kim, Jong Yeon Lee — arXiv:2610.12441**

The authors construct a short-range disordered model of $N$-component vector spins, with random matrix couplings on nearest-neighbor links and random tensor couplings on plaquettes. In the large-$N$ limit, it supports several replica-symmetry-breaking phases, including continuous Parisi hierarchies. Fluctuation analysis identifies different locality constraints: random anisotropy destabilizes uniform ferromagnetism for $D\leq4$, while gapless replica modes destabilize continuous replica-symmetry breaking for $D\leq2$ at leading order in $1/N$.

A further result gives the hierarchy a spatial interpretation. Two pure states with overlap $q$ have a characteristic correlation length $\xi(q)$ for differences between frozen magnetization patterns. Within a continuous hierarchy, this length is temperature-independent and decreases with overlap. These conclusions concern the designed large-component model and its controlled expansion; they do not settle the phase structure of generic finite-component short-range spin glasses. [v1 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12441v1)

**Background.** Replica-symmetry breaking organizes many glassy pure states by their overlaps. Ultrametricity means those states have a nested, tree-like organization. Establishing how that mean-field picture survives spatial locality remains a central theoretical problem.

**Why it matters for you:** This provides a broader connection to controlled many-body models, collective modes, and infrared instability. Translating an abstract state-space hierarchy into real-space correlation lengths makes the result especially useful for physical intuition.

[arXiv 2610.12441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12441)

## arXiv-2026-10-09 — Item 12

### Spin Chain with Continuous Exponential Symmetry: Topological Phase and Beyond

**Biao Lian, Zhen Bi — arXiv:2610.10682**

This paper studies spin-$S$ chains with an unusual continuous symmetry whose local charge unit varies exponentially along the chain, as $q^{L-j}$. The symmetry is $U(1)\rtimes\mathbb Z_2$, and the existence and topology of symmetric gapped states depend jointly on spin and the parity of the integer base $q$.

For integer spin, odd $q$ permits a Haldane-type symmetry-protected phase, while even $q$ admits no nontrivial SPT phase. For half-odd-integer spin, odd $q$ gives a Lieb–Schultz–Mattis obstruction to a unique symmetric gapped ground state. Even $q$ instead enforces what the authors call a “half-SPT” phase, with a protected mode on only one edge and only part of the usual string-order structure. These results rely on the specified exponentially modulated symmetry; they are not a classification of ordinary uniform-symmetry spin chains. [v1 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10682v1)

**Background.** An SPT phase has boundary properties protected by symmetry despite a gapped bulk. Haldane chains supply a familiar example. Lieb–Schultz–Mattis-type obstructions constrain whether symmetry and a unique gapped state can coexist; changing the spatial action of symmetry can change those constraints.

**Why it matters for you:** It expands the digest toward quantum many-body symmetry and boundary protection through a concrete model. The one-edge result is a useful counterexample to intuition developed from spatially uniform chains and suggests new connections between modulated charges and protected quantum degrees of freedom.

[arXiv 2610.10682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10682)
